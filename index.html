Research

Just transitions, from the community up

I am an Environmental Scientist focused on the complex challenges arising from energy transition in the global north. I have been trained in multi-disciplinary teams consisting of environmental scientists, geographers, political scientists, urban planners, and governance scholars. My proudest works have focused on community-focused efforts in ensuring Just Transitions, while my other, equally important works include transitions at state and county levels pertaining to renewable energy and electric vehicles.

My dissertation, Governance and Equity in Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure, asks how the institutions that fund, site, and regulate public charging shape who actually gets access to it. I am on the academic job market for positions beginning in 2027.

Manuscript in progress

Restructuring of Arizona's EV charging governance network

Arizona offers an unusual vantage point on charging infrastructure politics: buildout has been federally funded since 2009, led through the Arizona Corporation Commission, under a purple legislature and a moderate EV agenda — sitting between early adopters like California and New York and the lagging southern states.

Mapping the state-level governance network over time reveals four distinct periods of restructuring, each tracking fluctuations in nationally funded programs. The 2025 federal funding freeze registers as a major disruption to the network.

Manuscript Under Review

Distributional Inequalities in Maricopa County's Electric Vehicle Charging Infrastructure

This study is the first paper to come out of my dissertation and investigates the supply side of the EVCI landscape. In doing so, this study addresses an analytical gap regarding the spatial distribution of EVCI by incorporating residents' proximity to public charging options and the distribution of residents around chargers.

We develop a novel methodological metric "Population and Proximity Integrated Density " and compare distribution across socio-economic indicators against those produced by binary and count-based metrics. Ongoing

Arizona needs a structured energy transition funding mechanism

States such as Colorado, New Mexico, and Minnesota have laid down structured funding mechanisms that give communities facing coal plant closures a pathway to diversify their economies. Arizona has no such pathway, despite four coal plant closures leading up to 2030.

The JET Center is working with an advisory committee to build a set of policy recommendations for state policymakers, aimed at establishing Arizona's first dedicated transition funding mechanism. The project also produces a policy report drawing on transitions elsewhere in the U.S. — including outside the energy sector — alongside economic modelling of Northeastern Arizona's transition needs.
