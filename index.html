Research

Ethics, Policy & Social Implications of Health Screening

I am an systems scientist focused on the ethical, legal, social, and financial impacts of preventative screening. My current work focuses on newborn screening and patient trust, with a specialized focus in genomic sequencing potential.

My dissertation, "Newborn Screening Declination: Policies, Programs and Parents", asks how policies, programmatic capacity, and parental perspectives play a role in parental decision-making to have their child screened, as well as state's responsibilty to screen. I am on the academic job market for positions beginning in 2027.

Manuscript in preparation

Legal Mapping Policy Analysis: Newborn Screening Objections in the United States

Newborn Screening policies in the United States appear to rising for the first time in decades. My study attemtps to understand the language within state policies that mandate parents to refuse this public health measure for their child.

Mapping the state-level governance of newborn screening policies regarding when parents can refuse screening for their child, as well as external factors such as education, cost of screening, and bloodspot destruction policies.

  • Newborn Screening
  • Legal Epidemiology
  • Policy analysis
